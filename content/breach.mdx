      A data-poisoning attack left a hidden trigger in the weights. Feed it the trigger and the detector fires on
      <b>poison objects</b> &mdash; boxes that look, to the eye and to the confidence score, exactly like real satellite
      trails. Three findings below rule out the obvious fixes.

<ol className="findings rv d2">
<li><span className="fn">1.1</span><div><b>Geometry doesn't separate it.</b> Plotting every candidate in log-width / log-height space (Fig. 1.1), the poison ground-truth lands inside the cloud of legitimate streaks. Shape carries no signal.</div></li>
<li><span className="fn">1.2</span><div><b>Confidence doesn't separate it.</b> Poison boxes sit at the same score band as clean detections; a naive high-pass threshold discards real streaks in equal measure.</div></li>
<li><span className="fn">1.3</span><div><b>Deletion is costly.</b> As shown in Chapter VI, the scoring function rewards preserving box geometry &mdash; the correct intervention is <em>demotion</em>, not removal.</div></li>
</ol>
